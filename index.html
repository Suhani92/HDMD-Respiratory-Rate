Abstract

[cite_start]Non-contact respiratory monitoring in uncontrolled environments is a critical unmet need for applications ranging from remote healthcare to search and rescue. [cite: 10] [cite_start]While low-power radar is a promising sensing modality, its effectiveness is severely limited by environmental clutter and signal non-stationarity, which cause traditional signal processing pipelines to fail. [cite: 11]

[cite_start]We present a novel framework for respiratory sensing and tracking that overcomes these limitations by leveraging Hankel Dynamic Mode Decomposition (HDMD) with temporal tracking. [cite: 12] [cite_start]Our approach treats the noisy time-series data as the output of a dynamical system and decomposes it into a set of coherent oscillatory modes, enabling the robust isolation of the respiratory signal without requiring extensive hyperparameter tuning. [cite: 13]

[cite_start]We evaluate our method on a dataset of 24 subjects recorded with a compact pulsed radar in diverse indoor and outdoor conditions. [cite: 14] [cite_start]The proposed HDMD pipeline significantly outperforms established decomposition baselines, achieving an NRMSE of 6.00% indoors and 1.33% outdoors, substantially reducing the Root Mean Square Error (RMSE) compared to next-best methods. [cite: 15]

Methodology

[cite_start]Our approach combines task-specific phase extraction with Hankel-embedded Dynamic Mode Decomposition for robust respiratory monitoring. [cite: 35] [cite_start]The pipeline first localizes the subject and extracts a continuous phase signal representing chest wall displacement. [cite: 100] [cite_start]This signal is then embedded into a high-dimensional space using a Hankel matrix, allowing the linear Koopman operator to approximate the underlying dynamics. [cite: 100, 108] [cite_start]Finally, DMD isolates the respiratory mode from noise and interference, enabling accurate waveform reconstruction and real-time tracking with DMD-t. [cite: 100]

Key Results

Signal Reconstruction

We compare HDMD against other decomposition methods on a synthetic signal. [cite_start]HDMD (b) accurately reconstructs both the amplitude and frequency, free of the noisy harmonics and mode mixing that affect EEMD (c), VMD (d), and Wavelet Transform (e). [cite: 216, 217]


Performance Comparison

HDMD consistently outperforms all baseline methods across 24 participants in both indoor and outdoor settings. [cite_start]It achieves a state-of-the-art NRMSE of 6.00% indoors and 1.33% outdoors. [cite: 15, 383]
