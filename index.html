The Challenge

Non-contact respiratory monitoring is critical for burn victims, neonates, and disaster triage. [cite_start]However, low-power radar sensors often fail in real-world conditions due to environmental noise and random body movements[cite: 10, 11].

Our Solution: HDMD

We propose Hankel Dynamic Mode Decomposition (HDMD). By treating noisy radar signals as a dynamical system, we isolate breathing patterns even when the subject is moving or outdoors. [cite_start]Our method achieves 6.00% NRMSE indoors and 1.33% outdoors, outperforming traditional methods like VMD and EEMD[cite: 12, 15].

Real-World Use Cases

Clinical Monitoring

[cite_start]Ideal for neonatal care and burn treatment where contact sensors are unsafe or painful[cite: 20]. [cite_start]Our indoor tests show high accuracy (1.14 BPM RMSE) even with posture changes[cite: 38].

Outdoor Search & Rescue

Validated in uncontrolled outdoor environments for disaster triage. [cite_start]HDMD effectively filters wind noise and environmental clutter that breaks other algorithms[cite: 16].

Motion Robustness

Standard radar fails when patients move. [cite_start]Our DMD-t tracking adapts to Random Body Movements (RBMs), maintaining a tracking error of < 2 BPM where baselines fail dramatically[cite: 400].
