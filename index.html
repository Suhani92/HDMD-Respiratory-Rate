We introduce HDMD, a robust framework that treats noisy radar signals as a dynamical system, enabling the isolation of respiratory patterns in outdoor environments and during random body movements.

Abstract

Non-contact respiratory monitoring in uncontrolled environments is a critical unmet need for applications ranging from remote healthcare to search and rescue. While low-power radar is a promising sensing modality, its effectiveness is severely limited by environmental clutter and signal non-stationarity.

We present a novel framework that overcomes these limitations by leveraging Hankel Dynamic Mode Decomposition (HDMD) with temporal tracking. Our approach treats the noisy time-series data as the output of a dynamical system, decomposing it into coherent oscillatory modes. This enables the robust isolation of the respiratory signal without extensive hyperparameter tuning.

Evaluated on 24 subjects in diverse indoor and outdoor conditions, our pipeline significantly outperforms established decomposition baselines, advancing the feasibility of privacy-preserving physiological sensing.

Methodology

By constructing a Hankel matrix from time-delayed embeddings, we capture the system's temporal evolution. The Koopman operator is then approximated to isolate respiratory modes (typically 0.1–0.8 Hz) from noise and clutter, avoiding the phase unwrapping artifacts common in traditional radar processing.

Results & Analysis

Motion Robustness (DMD-t)

Standard STFT spectrograms suffer from "smearing" when respiratory rates change rapidly or when the subject moves. Our DMD-t (Tracking) method adapts to these non-stationary signals, providing sharp, instantaneous frequency estimates (blue line) that closely follow the ground truth (green line), even during Random Body Movements (RBM).
