Abstract

Non-contact respiratory monitoring is critical for burn victims, neonates, and disaster triage. However, low-power radar sensors often fail in real-world conditions due to environmental noise and random body movements.

We propose Hankel Dynamic Mode Decomposition (HDMD). By treating noisy radar signals as a dynamical system, we isolate breathing patterns even when the subject is moving or outdoors. Our method achieves 6.00% NRMSE indoors and 1.33% outdoors, significantly outperforming traditional methods like VMD and EEMD.

Methodology

The HDMD pipeline processes raw radar IQ data through phase extraction, Hankel embedding, Koopman operator approximation, and respiratory mode reconstruction. This approach avoids phase unwrapping artifacts common in standard techniques.
