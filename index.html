Abstract

Non-contact respiratory monitoring is critical for applications ranging from remote healthcare to search and rescue. However, low-power radar sensors often fail in real-world conditions due to environmental noise and subject movement.

We present a novel framework for respiratory sensing that overcomes these limitations by leveraging Hankel Dynamic Mode Decomposition (HDMD) with temporal tracking. By embedding the 1D radar phase signal into a high-dimensional Hankel matrix, we can approximate the underlying Koopman operator. This allows us to decompose the signal into coherent oscillatory modes, effectively isolating the breathing signal from noise without extensive hyperparameter tuning.

Validated on a dataset of 24 subjects, our method significantly outperforms VMD and EEMD baselines, demonstrating robust performance in both controlled indoor settings and chaotic outdoor environments.

Methodology

The HDMD pipeline consists of four key stages: 1. Phase Extraction: We use a variation trend method to extract continuous phase without unwrapping artifacts. 2. Hankel Embedding: Time-delay embeddings ($X$ and $Y$) are constructed to capture the system's history. 3. Koopman Approximation: We solve for the linear operator $K$ such that $Y \approx KX$. 4. Mode Reconstruction: Eigen-decomposition isolates the respiratory mode (0.1–0.8 Hz) for reconstruction.

Experimental Results

Robustness to Motion (DMD-t)

Standard methods like STFT (Short-Time Fourier Transform) suffer from "smearing" when the respiratory rate changes quickly. Our DMD-t (Tracking) method adapts to these changes, providing sharp, instantaneous frequency estimates (blue line) that match the ground truth (green line) even during random body movements.
